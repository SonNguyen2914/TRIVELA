# The First Prospective Slate — Whole-System Evaluation

**Slate: 2026-07-25, 15 MLS fixtures. Evaluated 2026-07-26.**

This is the first evidence the project has that was not generated by
looking backwards. Everything before it — the ladder, the walk-forward,
the +0.0331 edge estimate — was computed on fixtures that had already
happened, by a model whose constants were chosen with those fixtures in
view. These 15 were locked at T-10, before kickoff, under a frozen book,
by a model that could not be adjusted afterwards.

The conclusion is short: **the machinery held, the forecast said nothing,
and the evidence layer was lying.** All three deserve to be stated
plainly.

The strongest single result is the replay: a run written days earlier
reproduced **bit-exactly** from its stored artifact alone. That is the
property the whole evidence contract rests on, and it is now
demonstrated rather than asserted.

## 2. Forecast — no signal, and the slate could never have provided one

|  | log loss | Brier |
|---|---|---|
| Model (locked at T-10) | 0.9263 | 0.5462 |
| Baseline (league base rates) | 0.9236 | — |

**Model −0.0027 worse than baseline**, bootstrap 95% CI
**[−0.0755, +0.0770]** (20,000 resamples). Favourite hit 10/15.

Two facts matter more than that number:

- **The confidence interval is roughly 5× wider than the effect being
  chased.** A 15-match slate cannot resolve an edge of +0.03. This was
  never going to be informative, and it isn't. Reporting the point
  estimate without this would be the same error the project has already
  made twice.
- **The slate was a genuine outlier.** 11 of 15 home wins — 73% against
  a 45.7% league rate, P(≥11) = 0.029. The model averaged 43% home. It
  "lost" mainly by failing to predict a 1-in-35 home skew, which is not
  a defect.

The standing estimate, with these fixtures now folded in, moved from
**+0.0331 (n=162)** to **+0.0269 (n=177), CI [−0.0052, +0.0609]** — down,
consistent with the slate scoring marginally below baseline, and still
**not significant**.

## 3. Execution — the number that was a lie

### What the ledger said

> 27 signals · **0 fills** · 27 rejected `NET_EDGE_TOO_LOW`

The natural reading — and the one this document's first draft made — is
that *the model never disagreed with the market by more than 3%*. That
reading was wrong, and the ledger had no way to tell anyone.

### What had actually happened

`FEE_POLICY["version"]` is `"kalshi-fee-2026-07-general"`: 26 characters.
`paper_fill.fee_policy_version` was `String(24)`. PostgreSQL rejects
that; SQLite — the entire test suite's backend — does not enforce VARCHAR
length at all. So every `paper_fill` INSERT died in production while 500
tests stayed green.

`paper_trade_lock` wraps one lock in one transaction. Therefore:

- a lock whose signals were **all rejections** wrote no fill, and
  committed normally;
- a lock that produced **even one fill** hit the truncation, and the
  rollback discarded that lock's signals too.

The ledger retained **100% of the rejections and 0% of the fills.** The
six missing locks were not a random 40% — they were *precisely* the
fixtures where the model disagreed with the market strongly enough to
trade. The `paper.py` docstring claims the ledger "has no survivorship
bias." It had the maximum amount possible.

### How it was found

Not by inspection. By building the **denominator**: 15 locks × 3
quote-linked legs = 45 eligible, ledger held 27. The backfill written to
recover that gap reproduced the original production error verbatim, on
all six uncovered locks.

### What the slate actually produced

```text
signals   45        (was 27)
fills      7        (was 0)
rejected  38        all NET_EDGE_TOO_LOW
coverage  45/45     100%, 18 signals recovered and marked backfilled_at

settled   7 fills, all execution-grade (bounded_depth), 0 estimates
cost      $169.3237
P&L       −$69.3237
ROI       −40.94%
```

Payout was exactly **$100.00**, so exactly **one of seven fills hit**.

### Reading the −40.9% honestly

Average entry was **~23¢** — these were longshots. The seven fill
prices sum to **1.61 expected wins**; observed 1. Because the prices
differ, the correct distribution is Poisson-binomial, which gives
**P(≤1) = 0.49244.** Entirely ordinary variance.

> **Corrected 2026-07-26** (V9.5 independent evaluation). This first
> read 1.69 expected wins and P(≤1) = 0.465. 1.693237 is the total
> all-in COST per 100-contract equivalent — it includes $8.32 of fees —
> and fees move economic break-even, not the probability the market
> implies. The second error was using a single-p binomial across seven
> different prices. The conclusion is unchanged; the statistic was
> wrong and is fixed here.

```text
ROI as settled          −40.9%
ROI had ONE more hit    +18.1%     ← a single match flips the sign
ROI had one fewer hit   −100.0%
```

**The sign of this number is decided by one match.** It is not evidence
of edge in either direction. What it *is*: the first real execution
evidence the project has, replacing a fabricated zero with an honestly
bounded number.

## 4. What the three defects have in common

| # | Defect | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paper coverage | a numerator with no denominator |
| 2 | VARCHAR truncation | a green suite on a permissive backend, about a strict one |
| 3 | Engine signature | a lock's validity conflated with today's deployment state |

All three are the project's recurring failure: **a correct implementation
of an assumption never checked against reality.** Every line of code
involved did exactly what it said. The assumptions — *"a signal count is
readable on its own"*, *"the test database behaves like production"*,
*"a valid lock is one today's code can reproduce"* — were the parts
nobody had tested.

Defect #1 is literally the same shape as the DiskFull incident's
`{"created": 0}`: a count that reads as success purely because nothing
states what it should have been.

---

## 5. Verdicts

**Machinery: GO.** It did the job it was rebuilt four times to do, and
proved reproducibility on real frozen evidence.

**Profitability: NO-GO — unchanged.** n=15 fixtures and n=7 fills move
nothing. The edge estimate went *down* (+0.0331 → +0.0269) and its CI
still spans zero.

**Real money: NO-GO — unchanged.** `REAL_MONEY_SIGNALS_ENABLED=false`,
no kill switches engaged, no code path capable of enabling it.

---

## 6. What would actually move the verdict

Not another 15 fixtures. To resolve an effect of ~0.03 log loss against
the observed spread, the sample needs to be in the hundreds of scored
prospective fixtures — and the paper P&L needs enough *fills*, not
signals, to distinguish a real edge from seven longshots. On this
slate's rate (7 fills per 15 fixtures) that is a season's worth of
Saturdays, collected without touching the model.

The discipline that follows: **do not tune on this slate.** It is now
part of the scored sample (n=177), and the moment a constant is chosen
with it in view, it stops being prospective evidence and becomes another
in-sample fit.
